Video Overview

You are recommending one thing every golfer should add to their game. You have coached for twenty years and seen every kind of swing, so you know there is no single correct one. The thing they all have in common is where they arrive at impact. That is what you train, and the Slider is what you use to train it.

Video Structure

The one idea this video needs to land: your swing does not have to look like anybody else's, it just has to get to the right place at impact. Once a golfer believes that, the Slider is the obvious way to train it.

Your words, your style, but keep this order. The whole video should run 80 to 100 seconds. Reference clips sit inside each scene.

Scene 1 · The Hook (0 to 10 seconds)

Straight down the lens, on the course, no introduction: "If there is one thing I'd recommend every golfer add to his golf game, this would be it."

Do not say your name or your credentials yet. The recommendation comes first.

Scene 2 · Who You Are (10 to 25 seconds)

"I've been coaching twenty years. Thousands of lessons. And the first thing you learn is there's no one correct swing. As a coach, I've seen it all."

Then the line the whole video rests on: "Everyone swings it differently, but as long as you get the right impact position, you're going to make improvements in your game."

Say that second line exactly as written. Everything else is yours.

Scene 3 · The Three Swings (25 to 45 seconds). This is the video. Everything else is setup.

"Watch these three." Then three golfers, one after another, swinging. Pick three who look as different from each other as you can find — different builds, different tempos, different backswings.

"Different backswings. Different everything. Now watch where they all get to."

We freeze all three at impact side by side in the edit, so film each swing cleanly from the same angle. "Same position. That's the part that matters."

Film each of the three swings twice, face-on and down the line, so we can choose. The more different the three look, the better this works.

Scene 4 · The Slider (45 to 75 seconds)

"So how do you actually train it? This is the TRS Slider. Robert Rock designed it. I use it with my players and I use it myself."

Put it on while you talk: "Strap it on your trail arm, hit balls. It keeps your arm connected so you arrive in that position without thinking about it."

Then show the feedback: "You feel it straight away, go to throw the arm and it stops you." Hit a few shots with it on.

Talk through what you are feeling as you hit, the way you would with a student stood next to you. Not a product pitch.

Scene 5 · The Close (75 to 100 seconds)

"That's the feeling. That's all it is."

"Your swing's still your swing. You'll just start striking it better."

"There's a 60-day guarantee on it, so give it a go."

Also Capture

Range and course shots in your usual style, and a few close-ups of the strap on the arm. The vibe is the brand.

Production notes
Film outside on the range or the course, not indoors. Natural light.
Wear the Slider from Scene 4 onward. Scenes 1 to 3 are you and the swings.
The three swings in Scene 3 carry the whole video. Give that the most time.
Film clean swings. We add the freeze frames and any graphics in the edit.
Speak like you are coaching a student, not presenting to a camera.
Nothing needs memorising except the one line in Scene 2.

Why This Video

What works for us at the moment is coaches actually teaching on camera, so keep this as close to a real lesson as you can. Nothing about slicing in this one. Plenty of golfers who do not slice still have a bad impact position, and this video is for all of them. Some viewers will think "that will not work for my swing" — the three swings in Scene 3 are the answer to that, which is why they matter more than anything else here.
